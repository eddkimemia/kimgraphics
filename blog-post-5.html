The Hidden Costs of Cheap Web Design (And How to Avoid Them)

By Kim Graphics Team • April 20, 2024 • 7 min read

We see it all the time: a business owner in Nairobi tries to save money by hiring a "freelancer" or using a cheap automated builder for KSh 5,000. Six months later, they come to us because their site is broken, slow, or—worst of all—completely invisible on Google. "Cheap" web design often ends up being the most expensive mistake a company can make. Here's why.

1. The "Ghost" Developer Cost

Many low-cost providers build a site and then disappear. When you need to update your pricing, add a new service, or fix a bug that’s preventing M-Pesa payments, they aren't reachable. You end up having to pay a professional agency (like us) double to untangle a messy, poorly coded site. Professional support is part of the value of a premium build.

2. Security Vulnerabilities

Cheap sites often use pirated themes or outdated plugins. This makes your site a target for hackers. We've seen local Kenyan businesses lose years of customer data and damage their reputation because their "affordable" site was hijacked by malware. The cost of a security breach is 100x the cost of a secure website.

3. Zero Conversion Strategy

A designer might make a "pretty" site for a low price, but a pretty site that doesn't sell is just an expensive art project. Cheap sites lack clear Call-to-Actions (CTAs), lead magnets, and optimized sales funnels. If your site gets 1,000 visitors but 0 leads, it's a failure. A high-conversion site pays for itself within months.

4. Poor SEO Performance

Structure matters. Cheap sites are often built with heavy, bloated code that search engines hate. If your site doesn't load in under 3 seconds or isn't mobile-optimized for the Kenyan network, you will never rank on Google. You'll then have to spend more on ads to get the traffic that you should be getting for free.

Conclusion: Don't look at a website as an expense; look at it as an investment. A KSh 50,000 site that generates KSh 500,000 in revenue is much "cheaper" than a KSh 5,000 site that generates zero sales.
